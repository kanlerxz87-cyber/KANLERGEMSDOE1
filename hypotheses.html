First to investigate
ASTER hydrothermal mineral alteration		 ASTER Level-2 surface reflectance and surface emissivity; test continuum-removed Al-OH absorption in the SWIR and silica/quartz-related emissivity contrasts in the thermal bands. Target a spatially coherent alteration halo with a linear edge or corridor, not a single bright pixel. Hydrothermal alteration can mark fluid pathways above a blind or poorly exposed conduit even where no mappable fault scarp exists. This is indirect evidence for structure, not a direct fault label. Different sensor and physics from the reviewed DEM/LiDAR, potential-field, GeoDAWN radiometric, and point-temperature/paleo-geothermal approaches. ASTER is not named in the reviewed focus-site hypothesis slate; that is a limited search, not proof it was never tried elsewhere. Host-rock mineralogy, clay-rich basin sediment, mine tailings, and exposed lithologic contacts can mimic alteration spectra. USGS LP DAAC ASTER surface reflectance and ASTER surface emissivity. Product coverage, licensing terms, footprint completeness, and access were not checked in this session; NASA/USGS hosts are outside the active network allowlist. Blocked; not yet viable. Potential: high scientific relevance, unmeasured. Cost: high; scene selection, masking, co-registration, spectral preprocessing, and spatial validation.
Second
Persistent Landsat thermal–vegetation corridors		 Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 surface temperature plus surface reflectance/vegetation indices over multiple seasons. Target a persistent, narrow thermal/moisture anomaly aligned across dates, after masking cloud, topographic illumination, and known roads/irrigation. Permeable blind faults can focus warm groundwater and persistent soil-moisture or vegetation anomalies. Requiring temporal persistence could separate a stable fluid pathway from a one-date surface artifact. Uses continuous multi-date satellite observations, rather than sparse 2-m probe points or mapped paleo-geothermal sites described in the reviewed GEMSDOE32 work. It is not the same as a single thermal layer. Irrigation, canals, albedo changes, road/rail disturbance, playa brines, and terrain illumination can generate linear or persistent surface anomalies. USGS Landsat Collection 2. Coverage, usable acquisition density, and access were not checked in this session; the USGS host is outside the active network allowlist. Blocked; not yet viable. Potential: medium to high, highly confounder-dependent and unmeasured. Cost: high; time-series compositing and careful spatial/seasonal controls.
Third
Geologic-contact offset and termination network		 USGS digital geologic map unit polygons/contact traces plus the existing DEM only for geometric context. Detect paired contact terminations or consistent offsets across adjacent units along a shared corridor; exclude mapped fault linework as an input. Displaced stratigraphic contacts can preserve evidence for a structure hidden by subdued relief or omitted from a fault catalogue. The test would seek a cross-unit, geometrically consistent discontinuity rather than a generic lineament. Targets topology and cross-unit displacement in mapped geology, unlike scarp/ridge detectors and pixelwise magnetic, gravity, or conductivity gradients. Its novelty and coverage have not been audited across all sibling sites. Unconformities, facies boundaries, erosional channels, map-sheet seams, and cartographic generalization can resemble offsets. USGS National Geologic Map Database. Digital map coverage and usable source scale for the full competition footprint were not checked in this session; the USGS host is outside the active network allowlist. Blocked; not yet viable. Potential: moderate, uncertain because map coverage may be uneven. Cost: high; map harmonization, scale controls, topology, and provenance audit.
