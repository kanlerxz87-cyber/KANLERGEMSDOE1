## 1. The metric algebra (official, verified)

The organizer scores Distance-Weighted Tversky (α=0.2, β=0.8, 300 m triangular
kernel k(d) = max(1−d/300 m, 0)):
<https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/>

    DTI = TP_w / (α·(TP_w + FP_w) + β·|G|),   α = 0.2, β = 0.8

with TP_w = Σ over hidden-truth pixels of max nearby prediction credit (submodular
`max` inside 300 m) and FP_w = Σ_x p(x)·[1 − max_g k(d(x,g))] (additive, and
distance-discounted: a dot 100 m from truth pays only 0.2·(1−0.667) ≈ 0.067
marginal FP, a dot beyond 300 m pays the full 0.2).
Two consequences: (i) redundant predictions inside one kernel *add* 0.2·FP each while
contributing nothing once the truth pixel is already covered — sparsity on a fixed
support is strictly rewarded; (ii) only exact known-fault pixels are masked at
scoring (organizer forum #11516:
<https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/4>),
so a 1–2 px digitization halo around mapped faults pays FP tax unless hidden truth
happens to sit there.

## 3. Why 0.2778 is the family optimum (best explanation)

The champion is the *sparsest* H19-5-family raster carrying the *widest tested
catalogue collar*. Under §1's algebra, each thinning step deletes predictions whose
expected marginal TP (≈0 inside an already-covered kernel, or on the masked halo)
is below their 0.2 marginal FP cost. The dose–response (8 distinct rasters,
monotone within every nested pair) plus
the G36 graft-penalty control make "FP-denominator reduction on a high-recall ridge
support" the most parsimonious explanation. It also explains why G28's differently
thinned 37,660-dot set scores slightly less (0.2710): same budget, worse placement.

marginal TP exceeds 0.2·marginal-FP *relative to the champion's own margin*. Three
