PhD-level interpretation, bounded by evidence

Why might H33-B2 have reached the highest claim?

Short answer: the source evidence is consistent with a precision-oriented thinning of a dense, repeated linework family—not proof of a newly discovered geological signal. A public cross-site review describes a progression from the D2.8 family to a smaller r1/B2 subset, while the GEMSDOE32 page itself labels 0.2778 as OWNER-REPORTED and says it lacks a submission-page receipt linking that value to the raster hash.

The official metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index. It gives false positives coefficient 0.2 and false negatives coefficient 0.8, with a triangular distance kernel extending 300 m. For each ground-truth pixel, the true-positive contribution uses the best nearby prediction; false-positive costs accumulate over predicted pixels. Therefore, removing redundant nearby dots can reduce false-positive mass without reducing much true-positive credit. But if thinning removes predictions that cover real hidden faults, the larger false-negative weight can make the score worse.

DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2 × FPw + 0.8 × FNw + ε) · triangular support = 300 m

What this does not prove: no access to private labels or organizer receipt means we cannot attribute a 0.2778 score to any particular pruning operation, quantify the hidden TP/FP trade-off, or establish that the candidate represents a genuine fault discovery. The official public leaderboard page fetched on 2026-10-26 showed 0.4437 at rank 1; the prompt's earlier 0.3195/0.4413 values are stale snapshots. These public leaderboard values are not estimates for our model.

Answer to the score question

Can a future unique submission exceed 0.2778?

Possible in principle; not demonstrated here. The official leaderboard proves that public submissions have exceeded the historical 0.2778 claim, but does not show that this project can. A defensible attempt requires the actual competition grid and labels, the shared evaluator, a predeclared lane, leakage checks, a replicated spatial holdout win with its confidence interval, and strict uniqueness checks on both the surface and final dots. No numerical forecast is justified by the evidence available in this checkout.
