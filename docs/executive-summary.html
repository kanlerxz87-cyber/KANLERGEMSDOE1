PhD-level finding: why 0.2778 is high inside GEMSDOE, and why it is not the competition ceiling

The official metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index with α=0.2, β=0.8 and a 300 m triangular kernel (problem description). Known USGS/INGENIOUS faults are masked from scoring. Consequences:

  1. Recall is priced 4× precision. A missed hidden-fault pixel costs 0.8; a false alarm costs 0.2.
  2. TP is a max over the 300 m kernel, not a sum. Painting a thick line wastes FP. Sparse binary dots with spacing just under 300 m are the metric’s own currency.
  3. The 0–200 m collar around the public catalogue is almost pure FP on the initial prize round (those pixels are next to masked known faults, not next to the hidden test traces except at true tips/extensions).

GEMSDOE32’s nested family is the cleanest demonstration: H19-5 multiline (OWNER-REPORT 0.1922) → D2.8 thinning (0.2600) → B=1 prune (0.2708) → B=2 prune (0.2778). Two levers created the jump: kernel non-maximum decimation (~+0.068) and near-catalogue collar exclusion (~+0.018). That is metric geometry, not a new geological discovery.

The public leaderboard high is 0.4437 (xiaofanhu, 31 submissions, snapshot 2026-10-26). Beating 0.2778 therefore requires a different ranking field that still packs well, not another permutation of H19-5. Re-thinning H19-5 is a 3-px duplicate of the champion family and is forbidden by the lane protocol.

This session’s IEDR lane is that different field (independent seismicity × dilatation × magnetic HGM). It is unique. It slightly beats the champion on this hide-and-recover point estimate (0.00617 vs 0.00452) but the 95% CIs overlap and SGMC is worse. That is a negative result, and a negative result is a deliverable.
