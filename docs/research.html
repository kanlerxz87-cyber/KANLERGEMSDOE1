2. Mathematical & fold-local OOF proof of why `B=0 → B=1 → B=2` improved DTI

The official Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (DTI) is pooled across pixels with a 300 m ($R=3\text{ px}$) linear-decay triangular kernel $K(d) = \max(0, 1 - d/3)$, false-positive weight $\alpha = 0.2$, and false-negative weight $\beta = 0.8$:

$$\mathrm{DTI}(P, G_{\text{hid}}) = \frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP} + 0.2\,\mathrm{FP} + 0.8\,\mathrm{FN}}, \quad \mathrm{TP} = \sum_{g \in G_{\text{hid}}} K\big(d(g, P)\big), \quad \mathrm{FP} = \sum_{p \in P \setminus \mathcal{C}} \Big(1 - K\big(d(p, G_{\text{hid}})\big)\Big)$$

As confirmed by the competition organizers, known USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue pixels $\mathcal{C}$ are masked pixel-exactly when scoring against hidden ground-truth faults $G_{\text{hid}}$. Why then does removing candidate dots within $1 \le d(\mathbf{x}, \mathcal{C}) \le 2\text{ px}$ of mapped faults $\mathcal{C}$ improve DTI?

  1. 300 m kernel redundancy along tips & splays: Any hidden fault segment $g \in G_{\text{hid}}$ that branches from or continues a mapped fault $\mathcal{C}$ receives kernel credit $K(d) = 1 - d/3 > 0$ from predicted dots placed at $d(p, \mathcal{C}) \in (2, 3]\text{ px}$. Meanwhile, the 6,436 dots of D2.8 that hug the immediate 1–2 px flank of mapped faults along their entire mapped length where no hidden branch exists each incur a full $\alpha = 0.2$ false-positive penalty in the denominator $(0.2 \times 6,436 = 1,287.2$ denominator units).
  2. Why full-map diagnostic evaluation (`0.004515`) was misleading vs fold-local OOF (`0.099202`): When GEMSDOE32.tif is evaluated as a static raster on GEMSDOE32-HIDE-RECOVER-v3.1, its pre-baked $B=2$ collar was computed against the entire catalogue $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}_{\text{vis}} \cup G_{\text{hid}}$, which carves a 200 m hole around the very withheld segments $G_{\text{hid}}$ being tested (`diagnostic_only = 0.004515`).
