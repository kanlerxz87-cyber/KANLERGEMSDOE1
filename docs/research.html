What the raster audit actually established

We compared independently pinned binary supports on the mirrored grid against the mapped catalogue. The 121,131-cell H19-5 support contains every positive cell of the 44,090-cell GEMSDOE25/30 support; the latter's minimum nearest-neighbor distance is √8 pixels (about 283 m). The two GEMSDOE25 and 30 supports are identical. Removing D2.8 cells at Euclidean distance ≤1 pixel from the mirrored fault catalogue yields exactly the 40,199-cell GEMSDOE31 support; removing those at distance ≤2 pixels yields exactly the 37,654-cell GEMSDOE32 support. Both XOR counts are zero. These measurements establish the nested supports and spacing, not the original thinning algorithm or the cause of a reported score.

Why pruning can help, and when it can hurt

The official DTI is pooled over pixels, with a triangular 300 m kernel, α=0.2 and β=0.8. Each truth pixel receives its best nearby probability×kernel credit; each predicted pixel contributes false-positive mass according to its distance to truth. Extra adjacent predictions can add little truth credit while increasing false-positive mass. Sparsifying or pruning poorly targeted cells can therefore improve DTI.

The organizer masks only known-fault pixels exactly, not a 200 m neighborhood, and hidden truth may lie nearby. A B=2 exclusion is a candidate-design choice: it can also discard good new faults. We cannot determine its causal effect without score-to-file receipts and the private truth. The public board showed rank 1 at 0.4544 on 2026-10-27. The user prompt states 0.3195; the discrepancy is unresolved. Neither board value confirms the score of h33.
